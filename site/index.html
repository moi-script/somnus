See what your nights actually looked like.

Somnus pairs a sensor band on your wrist with a radar by your bed. The app shows your pulse, skin response and movement, how long you were in the room each night, and what your bedroom light was doing while you slept.

Three pieces, one picture

Each piece does one job. The phone ties the band to the server; the room unit reports on its own over Wi-Fi.

The band

Pulse and blood oxygen, skin response, motion and steps. It buzzes on a fall, and the phone keeps its readings while you're offline.

The room unit

A 24 GHz radar by the bed that switches a smart bulb, and records when the room was in use and what the light was doing all night.

The app

Live readings, each night as a timeline, two weeks side by side, and controls for the bedroom light from your phone.

What it won't pretend to know

A health number nobody measured is worse than an empty space. Where the hardware can't tell, the app says so.

  • In the room, not asleepThe radar senses the room. It can't tell sleep from lying awake, so the app never claims to.
  • Gaps stay gapsIf the room unit loses Wi-Fi, that time is shown as missing. Nothing is filled in.
  • Light, stated as fact"22 minutes of cool light after 23:00", not a sleep score.
  • Not a medical deviceA student project for exploring your own data, not for diagnosis.

Installing it

  1. 1

    Download on your phone

    Open this page on your Android phone and tap Download. The file lands in Downloads.

  2. 2

    Allow this source

    Android blocks apps from outside the Play Store. Follow its Settings shortcut to allow your browser, then come back.

  3. 3

    Install and grant Bluetooth

    The app asks for Bluetooth and location. Android needs both to find the band; the app never reads your position.

Try Somnus

Android 6 or newer. You'll need the band or the room unit to see your own data.
